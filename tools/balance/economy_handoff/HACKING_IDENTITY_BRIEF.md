# Design direction: make access and information worth fighting over

## Player concern and decision

The player reports that RIM lacks both hacking and cyberpunk identity, alongside the earlier complaints about an economic core supporting endless expansion, full-farm hosts resisting attacks, and a small territorial lead deciding the match.

The recommended player promise is: **I run an underground hacker network. I discover openings in powerful organizations, use access to complete valuable operations, and decide who benefits from the information.**

This is a proposed direction to test. The finite-assault task in `CLAUDE_PROMPT.md` tests military commitment and counterattack. Success there would not establish this broader identity.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- Confirmed in the active rules: scanning reveals host information; cards can steal output, disable routing/production, capture hosts, and wipe installations. These are useful existing foundations.
- Confirmed: the main victory conditions reward majority ownership held for a duration or capturing an opposing rig. Farms, garrisons, and permanent sending channels support that territorial loop.
- Supported inference: the dominant decisions therefore resemble commanding an army across a network. Hacking terminology does not guarantee that discovering and exploiting access is a useful alternative to conquest.
- Confirmed: the repository contains corporate and resident lore generators. Their integration into the active Botnet experience has not been established by this review.
- Unknown: whether players would prefer operations, whether those operations remain competitive against expansion, and whether the city consequences would matter to them. No playtest result is claimed.

## One small experiment: steal a corporate debt ledger

Use one prepared scenario with an operator and an opposing security player or agent. The objective is to extract evidence of fraudulent debts from a corporate database. Give the database a named corporation and one affected resident or community, with a short visible consequence. Avoid a new campaign, reputation tree, or collection of unrelated hacking minigames.

The scenario should offer two understandable approaches to the same objective:

1. A direct route protected by a substantial garrison, where military commitment can matter.
2. A service route whose temporary access opportunity can be discovered through reconnaissance. Obtaining that access costs resources and requires a reachable foothold, but does not require defeating every bot at the database.

The critical distinction is **access is different from ownership**. Temporary access permits the operation; it does not transfer buildings, generate free farms, reveal everything, or give unrestricted control of the target network.

The player sequence is:

- **Discover:** learn a useful route and its access requirement. Recon must change the available plan, not merely fill a progress bar before the same attack.
- **Enter:** commit the required resources to temporary, scoped access. Reuse existing resource types where they fit. The access requirement must concern this target or route, so a large generic Credentials bank does not become a universal bypass.
- **Extract:** move the evidence back through a connected route. Show actual extraction progress, the remaining route, and the consequence of interruption. Information is secured when delivered; viewing the database is not success.
- **Respond or abort:** extraction creates a visible warning and gives security a chance to identify and isolate the compromised route. Isolation must have a local service or routing cost so permanent isolation is not automatically the best defense. Specify what each side knows; do not give the defender invisible omniscience.
- **Use the evidence:** sell it to a rival for Crypto, or publish it to gain temporary access to one community relay. The first funds operations; the second changes a future route. Both outcomes should be understandable before choosing. Copying or publishing a ledger does not itself erase debts from the source database.

The final choice is a proposed small, observable city consequence. It should identify who gains power and who is exposed, rather than present an unexplained good/evil score. If the relay reward requires too much new machinery, reduce the scenario scope before inventing a reputation system.

## Strategic purpose and tradeoff

A smaller network could accomplish something valuable by finding and maintaining an opening. A full garrison would remain useful against capture but would not answer every kind of intrusion. The larger player would retain more resources and routes, while needing to decide what to protect and when isolation is worth its cost.

This is a hypothesis, not an automatic comeback rule. A richer player might also dominate operations; prepared comparisons must test that possibility. Temporary access without readable detection and interruption would simply replace one unstoppable strategy with another.

For this isolated scenario, extraction versus containment is the objective. Do not silently add operation rewards while leaving majority control as the only meaningful measure of success. Conversely, do not replace the main game's victory rules as part of the finite-assault experiment. Decide how operations would contribute to a full match only after the small scenario shows useful decisions.

## What Claude should specify next

After reporting the combat experiment, return a concrete specification for this separate operation prototype: actions and eligibility, information available to each side, access expiry, warning and response rules, extraction interruption/recovery, success/failure, and the two outcome effects. Identify the minimum additions and what existing behavior can be reused. Treat costs and timing as provisional until checked against current production, travel, and response times.

Do not present extra neon, terminal text, card renaming, or additional lore as evidence that the interaction problem is solved. Presentation should make the operator's actions and the city's stakes legible.

## Test that decides whether to continue

Compare this scenario with the existing conquest loop. Use both a local opening and an appropriately defended layout, including the earlier 10-host-versus-12-host comparison with sides swapped. Record decisions and outcomes rather than assuming a smaller player must win.

Continue only if the experiment provides evidence that:

- Discovering information changes a player's route or plan.
- Temporary access enables useful progress without owning most of the map.
- Security can recognize and interrupt the operation through an understandable action.
- The operator has a meaningful decision to continue, reroute where available, or abort.
- Full farms, hoarded resources, and repeated operation attempts do not bypass those choices.
- Players can explain what they accomplished, who benefited, and what they risked.

Record failures too: a mandatory sequence of clicks, an unavoidable timer, an extraction that defenders cannot affect, or a reward that everyone ignores would reject the proposed mechanism. Automated scenarios can check rules and response windows; player observation is needed to assess the hacking fantasy and the city consequence.
