# Twenty-four small-town profiles: Netherlands, France, and UK

Prepared 4 October 2026. Read [SMALL_TOWN_ANALYSIS.md](../../SMALL_TOWN_ANALYSIS.md) for the combined algorithm, six proposed town families, and twenty improvements. See the [population register](POPULATION_SOURCES.md) for the under-50,000 selection, reference dates, and boundary caveats.

These profiles identify useful relationships between centres, housing, work, shopping, streets, and landscape. They are qualitative case studies, not complete zoning inventories. Descriptions concern the existing or historical structure documented in the sources; future interventions are labelled. Each **RIM rule** is our design inference, not a planning standard or a recovered historical algorithm.

## Netherlands

### 1. Gorinchem — historic centre, housing extensions, and work areas

The city's planning framework distinguishes the inner city from residential neighbourhoods including Gildenwijk, Stalkaarsen, Haarwijk, and Molenvliet. Its economic vision discusses established business areas, subdivision of larger business premises, and demand for smaller or mixed work locations. This gives the town several employment and residential settings beyond its recognisable historic centre. The environmental vision's 2050 ambitions should be read separately from its descriptions of the existing city. [Municipal environmental vision](https://www.gorinchem.nl/bestuur-organisatie/de-gorcumse-omgevingsvisie/), [economic vision](https://www.gorinchem.nl/files/2023-07/Economische%20visie%20Gorinchem_2021.pdf).

**RIM rule:** reserve a compact older centre, then attach coherent residential expansions and a separate accessible work area. Represent history through a few retained routes and edges; keep space for ordinary housing beyond the landmark core.

### 2. Culemborg — three different kinds of centre

The economic vision identifies the main centre, neighbourhood shopping at Parijsch/Koopmansgildeplein and Chopinplein, and maker activity around the Gelderlandfabriek. Pavijen supplies a different employment setting. The proposed 2040 direction transforms Pavijen I towards a station district while retaining work functions in other parts; the complete transformation is not an existing condition. [Economic vision](https://omgevingsvisie.culemborg.nl/onze-koers-richting-2040/economie/detail), [Pavijen vision](https://www.culemborg.nl/visie-op-pavijen).

**RIM rule:** distinguish main shopping, local daily services, and employment. A neighbourhood grocery cluster and a station workshop should not become identical downtown districts. Include an optional mixed redevelopment site while retaining the work area's larger plot structure.

### 3. Wageningen — institution-led town

The adopted 2026 vision identifies knowledge activity at the campus, Business & Science Park Wageningen, and downtown, alongside work areas with different requirements. It also addresses connections between these places and the rest of town. Its future land-use intentions do not mean every proposed mixed-use development has been built. [Adopted municipal vision](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR764733/1), [municipal adoption announcement](https://www.wageningen.nl/nieuws/omgevingsvisie-wageningen-vastgesteld/).

**RIM rule:** allow a relatively large campus/employment compound to coexist with a modest shopping centre and ordinary housing. Reserve institutional grounds and pedestrian connections. Put compatible small work uses near homes; provide a distinct site for activities needing separation or servicing.

### 4. Enkhuizen — old harbour town and later residential growth

The municipal vision describes fortifications, harbour areas, the Boerenhoek's agricultural history within the old town, a concentrated retail centre, northern residential neighbourhoods, and work locations including Krabbersplaat and Schepenwijk. This is a varied town with several functional areas. Proposed residential expansion and policies for reducing retail vacancy are future directions. [SED municipalities' Enkhuizen area description](https://www.omgevingsvisiesed.nl/de-bestaande-waarden/enkhuizen).

**RIM rule:** combine a compact historic shopping spine, substantial residential land, and one working harbour edge. Let a green or former defensive boundary influence the outline. Use dry residential blocks to establish the town before introducing harbour water.

### 5. Harlingen — residential centre and working waterfront

Harlingen treats its historic centre as a place for living, shopping, and visiting. The centre project seeks changes to streets and squares, while the environmental vision considers the wider port and employment role. Public-realm works scheduled from autumn 2026 and the longer-term vision are planning evidence, not proof of a completed future layout. [Municipal centre project](https://www.harlingen.nl/centrum), [environmental vision](https://www.harlingen.nl/omgevingsvisie).

**RIM rule:** give the waterfront different functions: a public centre edge with shops and housing, and a serviced work edge with yards or sheds. Keep homes and their grounds on a substantial continuous land area; imply the larger sea and port beyond the scene.

### 6. Doesburg — identifiable housing growth episodes

Doesburg's history describes the constraints imposed by fortifications, the arrival of a foundry, and successive residential development in Molenveld, De Ooi, and Beinum. Its historic centre remains a concentration of shops, hospitality, and cultural destinations. Present-day municipal material also identifies businesses in Beinum West and East. [Municipal history](https://www.doesburg.nl/geschiedenis-van-de-gemeente), [historic centre](https://www.doesburg.nl/historische-binnenstad), [business-area newsletter](https://www.doesburg.nl/_flysystem/media/zomereditieondernemersnieuwsbrief.pdf).

**RIM rule:** create a small older core and a few larger housing ensembles from different eras. Reserve an ordinary employment site as well. Use an inherited edge or route to explain where one development episode ends and the next begins.

### 7. Lochem — landscape-constrained and off-centre

The 2022 spatial analysis places industry north of the Twentekanaal and describes housing in relation to the Berkel, the Lochemse Berg, and the western provincial road. The historic centre occupies an uneven position within the developed town. The subsequent centre program seeks a compact, viable shopping area; proposed extensions and centre interventions must remain distinguished from existing form. [Housing vision and spatial analysis](https://www.lochem.nl/fileadmin/internet-doc/Wonen-Leven-Vervoer/visie/TOE_WOONVISIE_LOCHEM-ZWIEP.pdf), [centre plan](https://www.lochem.nl/ondernemen/centrumplan-lochem).

**RIM rule:** allow the historic centre to sit away from the geometric middle. Grow housing along selected accessible directions, preserve a green constraint, and attach work to its appropriate route. Do not interpret the municipality's 34,213 residents as the population of this town alone.

### 8. Leusden — planned housing and daily shopping nodes

The municipal vision describes two shopping centres, Hamershof and Biezenkamp, surrounded by residential neighbourhoods dominated by family homes, with business areas at the edges. Historic routes, estates, and landscape remain part of its identity. Policies to intensify centres or mix more functions into neighbourhoods are intentions for future change. The municipality describes Leusden as a village; it is included for its relevant planned-town form. [Leusden environmental vision](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR756706/1).

**RIM rule:** support a town without a mandatory medieval core. Compose green residential ensembles around everyday services. A second local centre should depend on the housing layout and available capacity, while edge workplaces remain connected to the same settlement.

## France

### 9. Bayeux — visitor centre with everyday shopping

Bayeux's commerce page distinguishes central shops from larger retail locations such as the Émeraude area and Carrefour. This is useful evidence that a visitor-oriented historic centre and daily consumer needs can have different spatial expressions. The municipal heritage material also identifies the cathedral as a major destination. [Municipal commerce guide](https://www.bayeux.fr/fr/economie-et-emploi/commerces), [municipal city website and heritage links](https://www.bayeux.fr/).

**RIM rule:** give a historic landmark and its shopping streets a compact shared setting. Surround it with ordinary homes and usable grounds. Where capacity permits, reserve a separate grocery/retail site with its own access; visitor-oriented frontage should not replace all everyday services.

### 10. Fougères — terrain, historical functions, and industrial reuse

The public tourism office describes a lower town around the castle and Saint-Sulpice, and an upper town with Saint-Léonard, the town hall, and belfry. Early craft activity used the Nançon setting. Municipal archives document a later shoe-manufacturing layer and the reuse of the Réhault factory by associations. [Historic structure](https://www.destination-fougeres.bzh/la-destination/passion-patrimoine/plongez-dans-laventure-medievale/), [factory history and reuse](https://fougeres.fr/lactualite/le-mardi-des-archives-lhistoire-des-ateliers/).

**RIM rule:** preserve two differently shaped older areas linked by a deliberate route, then add a work site and later housing. A small river or landscape constraint can explain the difference. Express a reused factory through its retained larger footprint and changed activity.

### 11. Saumur — connected quarters with different histories

The municipal historical routes distinguish districts including Fenêt and Saint-Nicolas, connecting the river, castle, religious buildings, trading streets, and other institutions. The Saint-Nicolas heritage route describes a quarter with a layered building history. These sources explain the historic town's differentiated quarters; they are not a complete survey of current suburban land uses or employment. [Municipal historical routes](https://www.ville-saumur.fr/images/pages/2024/Conseil_des_Sages/PARCOURS_HISTORIQUES_SAUMUR.pdf), [Saint-Nicolas route](https://www.ville-saumur.fr/suivre-la-signaletique-patrimoine-du-quartier-saint-nicolas).

**RIM rule:** use several connected neighbourhood identities with different anchors and block arrangements. Keep the river at a composed edge where possible, and reserve dry residential fabric behind the landmark frontage. A distinctive institution can occupy one larger compound.

### 12. Libourne — planned historic geometry and neighbourhood retail

Libourne's heritage account describes the bastide's planned geometry beside an earlier settlement and river-port activity. Its municipal commerce policy explicitly covers the historic Cœur de Bastide and several neighbourhood commercial sectors, illustrating a hierarchy beyond a single centre. The ongoing Cœur de Bastide program aims to improve housing and public space. [Municipal heritage history](https://www.libourne.fr/fileadmin/medias/Arborescence/Culturelle/Patrimoine/108132_Mairie_Libourne_Gazette_150x210-AR_16pages_v4.pdf), [central and neighbourhood commerce](https://www.libourne.fr/mon-quotidien/commerces/perimetre-de-sauvegarde-du-commerce-et-de-lartisanat-de-proximite), [renewal program](https://www.libourne.fr/decouvrir-libourne/coeur-de-bastide).

**RIM rule:** permit a deliberate local grid around a square, then vary the outer growth and frontage. Regular streets become convincing when they serve a specific neighbourhood structure and meet different surrounding patterns.

### 13. Pontarlier — mixed approaches and specialised outer sites

The 2026 planning report distinguishes the historic centre, functionally mixed faubourgs and central approaches, less dense residential areas, public facilities, and economic sites for activities that do not fit the central fabric. These categories describe and regulate different spatial forms; they are not measured land-use shares. The Agora des Remparts proposal combines housing, a media library, public space, and pedestrian continuity on a redevelopment site. [PLUi-H explanatory report](https://data.geopf.fr/annexes/gpu/documents/DU_242500338/4d81c730bfd6319f4945d07d72760d26/242500338_rapport_3_20260226.pdf), [Agora project](https://www.ville-pontarlier.fr/page/vivre-a-pontarlier/vos-quartiers/agora-des-remparts).

**RIM rule:** transition from central mixed frontage through mixed approach streets to quieter housing. Give larger work/retail buildings complete sites. One civic redevelopment can connect paths through an established block.

### 14. Vitré — rail-connected centre and employment districts

The town describes a railway connection in its centre, central shops, and two weekly food markets on Place de la République. Intercommunal economic planning identifies business areas, including the Briquetterie extension, with specific road connections. The latter is evidence of a planned employment layout; the cited 2020 document does not establish the completion of every phase. [Municipal commerce page](https://www.vitre.bzh/commercants), [Briquetterie planning document](https://www.vitrecommunaute.org/medias/2020/01/Arrete-municipal.pdf).

**RIM rule:** locate a modest transport anchor next to, or on a direct route to, the mixed centre. Reserve the market square as usable public space. Give a larger employment site outward service access while keeping it reachable from residential neighbourhoods.

### 15. Dax — specialist institutions within a functioning town

Dax's municipal material identifies thermal activity as a defining function. Its investment and centre pages distinguish central retail, the station quarter, hotel reuse, and public-space improvement projects. These give the town several kinds of destination rather than a single office-led centre. Announced redevelopment remains an intention unless delivery is explicitly documented. [Municipal thermal activity overview](https://www.dax.fr/dax/bien-etre-et-thermalisme/thermalisme/), [centre and investment locations](https://www.dax.fr/dax/entreprendre/investir-en-centre-ville/).

**RIM rule:** give a specialist institution a complete compound and let related accommodation/services cluster nearby. Retain ordinary residential neighbourhoods and grocery access. Institutional size can reflect a wider catchment without forcing the entire surrounding settlement to be dense.

### 16. Sarlat-la-Canéda — old centre, later route, and wider catchment

The municipality describes Sarlat as a commercial centre for its surrounding territory, with shops and markets concentrated in central streets and squares. Its heritage guide explains the later Traverse, now Rue de la République, and identifies neighbourhood life beyond the oldest centre, including Pontet. [Commerce and markets](https://sarlat.fr/mon-quotidien/commerces-et-marches/), [municipal heritage guide](https://www.sarlat.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/FOCUS_sarlat-def.pdf).

**RIM rule:** allow one later through street to cross an older pattern, retaining smaller spaces and quieter residential frontage around it. Visitor/market demand can strengthen a compact centre. Reserve housing and daily services beyond its most decorative streets.

## United Kingdom

### 17. Wells, England — cathedral precinct and market/high-street centre

Wells City Council identifies its cathedral, Bishop's Palace, and other historic civic places. The neighbourhood plan considers the High Street, central shopping, market activity, visitor destinations, and residential development together. Its future housing-site design options are proposals rather than a record of completed estates. [City Council overview](https://www.wells.gov.uk/about), [Neighbourhood Plan](https://www.wells.gov.uk/userfiles/files/Neighbourhood%20Plan%20Files/Wells%20Neighbourhood%20Plan%20March%202023.pdf).

**RIM rule:** distinguish the landmark's grounds from the shopping frontage and market square, while connecting them on foot. Attach residential streets with gardens and an everyday service site. A low-rise landmark and readable public space can establish the whole centre's identity.

### 18. Frome, England — local character at neighbourhood scale

Frome's adopted Town Design Statement identifies 27 character areas and addresses settlement edges, the river corridor, gardens, frontage, shopfronts, housing groups, and the railway-station gateway. It distinguishes existing local character from guidance for future construction. The relevance is the scale of coordination: even a small town requires multiple related arrangements rather than one townwide block template. [Town Design Statement and council summary](https://www.frometowncouncil.gov.uk/council/frome-town-council/planning-for-frome/frome-town-design-statement/).

**RIM rule:** assign each housing ensemble a compatible street, plot, garden, and building family. Use a small number of these ensembles at RIM's scale. Make the settlement edge and station approach part of the layout rather than decorative leftovers.

### 19. Ludlow, England — market centre with a wider service role

Shropshire Council describes the historic centre on an escarpment above the River Teme within a countryside setting. Castle Square hosts a regular market. Its town-centre audit records employment and transport connections as well as residents, reinforcing the distinction between town population and its economic role. [Council place plan](https://next.shropshire.gov.uk/place-plans/place-plan-projects-by-area/ludlow-and-surrounding-area/), [Castle Square market](https://next.shropshire.gov.uk/property-services/markets-in-shropshire/ludlow/), [2023 audit](https://www.shropshire.gov.uk/media/26828/ludlow-town-centre-audit-report-2023.pdf).

**RIM rule:** reserve a market square at a meaningful focal point, retain a clear countryside edge, and connect shops/services to outward routes. A service-town centre can have importance beyond the homes visibly represented on the map.

### 20. Letchworth Garden City, England — planned green housing with work

The conservation appraisal distinguishes the town centre and residential areas, while the residential design guide emphasises their planned character. The council's economic strategy records substantial office and industrial premises and manufacturing activity, as well as the centre's changing role. Letchworth's green housing therefore belongs to a wider town program that includes employment. [Conservation appraisal](https://www.north-herts.gov.uk/sites/default/files/letchworth_conservation_area_appraisal.pdf), [residential design guide](https://www.north-herts.gov.uk/sites/default/files/files/spg20_letchworth_design_guide_oct_2000.pdf), [economic portrait](https://democracy.north-herts.gov.uk/documents/s28978/Appendix%20A%20-%20North%20Hertfordshire%20Economic%20Strategy%20-%20Final.pdf).

**RIM rule:** coordinate houses, gardens, green corridors, and local streets around a deliberately placed centre. Reserve a separate work area with suitable access. Green character requires connected useful space and consistent domestic frontage.

### 21. Stirling, Scotland — constrained old core and later avenues

Stirling City Heritage Trust describes the royal burgh growing on slopes below the castle, with long narrow plots, followed by nineteenth-century villas in larger grounds along tree-lined streets. Broad Street was a trading and administrative focus; retained lanes provide another movement scale. This offers an explanation for visibly different forms within one town. [Historic street development](https://www.stirlingcityheritagetrust.org/blog/avenues-past-stirlings-historic-streets-exhibition), [Broad Street history](https://www.stirlingcityheritagetrust.org/explore-historic-stirling/broad-street).

**RIM rule:** attach deeper plots and narrow frontage to the older centre; use larger grounds and quieter streets for a later expansion. Preserve pedestrian links between them. Keep the NRS locality, wider settlement, and large council area distinct when interpreting scale.

### 22. St Andrews, Scotland — long streets, institutions, and smaller links

Fife's conservation appraisal explains the historic development of North Street and South Street, their eastward relationship to the cathedral, and Market Street's emergence from an intermediate back street. The document also records institutional buildings and their grounds. It is a historical morphology source; its warning says the conservation-area boundary changed after publication. [Fife conservation appraisal](https://www.fife.gov.uk/__data/assets/file/0027/93582/St-Andrews-Final_With_Warning-102023.pdf).

**RIM rule:** keep sustained street frontage and deeper intervening plots, with selected small connections rather than junctions at every possible interval. Give institutional grounds their own footprint within the town. A later market street can acquire a different use from the streets on either side.

### 23. Aberystwyth, Wales — coastal centre and institutional anchors

The council-hosted 2024 draft Place Plan describes a town between hills, a seafront and harbour, shops and housing, and employment associated with the university and National Library. It addresses neighbouring communities as well as the town, and its project list contains future proposals. Its headline population boundary is unresolved in this research; see the population register. [Draft Place Plan](https://www.ceredigion.gov.uk/media/n1dldqae/2024-aberystwyth-place-plan.pdf).

**RIM rule:** compose a compact centre along one coastal edge and place institutions on connected landward sites. Use landscape reserves to steer housing growth. Keep most sea outside the scene and preserve ordinary homes and their grounds between major destinations.

### 24. Enniskillen, Northern Ireland — island centre and regional shopping

The council's baseline study describes the island setting, a retail spine along Darling Street, Church Street, Townhall Street, and East Bridge Street, and other shopping locations including Erneside. It identifies a wider catchment that includes cross-border shopping. The Place Shaping Plan's 2035 projects are future proposals. [Council plan and baseline documents](https://www.fermanaghomagh.com/services/communityplanning/place-shaping-in-fermanagh-and-omagh/enniskillen-place-shaping-plan/), [baseline analysis, particularly retail section](https://www.fermanaghomagh.com/services/communityplanning/place-shaping-in-fermanagh-and-omagh/enniskillen-place-shaping-plan/?download=file&file=72363).

**RIM rule:** use this as an optional specialised setting. Reserve a substantial connected centre, deliberate crossings, complete bridge approaches, and housing on usable land. The retail spine and supporting destinations explain activity; the water must leave room for the settlement to function.

## What the comparison supports

The transferable patterns are a centre with connected frontage, differentiated housing episodes, sites shaped by their work or institutional use, selective local services, inherited routes, and meaningful landscape edges. Each country's examples contain exceptions to simple national stereotypes. A town generator should combine these relationships according to role, history, terrain, and scene capacity, then apply a coordinated architectural treatment.

The profiles do not establish precise current land-use percentages, an optimal road density, or national probabilities for malls, campuses, rivers, or apartment blocks. Those would require a separate measured GIS study with consistent settlement boundaries. The immediate RIM work can proceed using explicit prototype settings and rendered comparisons, keeping measured results separate from these qualitative inferences.
