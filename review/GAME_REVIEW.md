**RIM v0.5.6 — expert game design, playability and accessibility review**

Reviewed 2 October 2026, Europe/Berlin. Deliverables: **50 bug/defect findings, 50 improvement tweaks, 20 new feature ideas**.

This review combines three delegated specialist reviews (game design/economy, UX/accessibility, and simulation/automation) with direct source inspection and Godot 4.7.2 headless probes. The working version advanced from 0.5.4 to 0.5.6 during review. The final main/UI implementations were compared with the tested snapshot; recommendations account for the existing contracts, sweeps, alerts, tooltips and Cash graph. The design documents still describe older mechanics.

**The principal judgement**

RIM's strongest identity is a cyberpunk network factory: the city supplies targets, packets carry goods, Cycles sustain the network, and routing creates the optimization puzzle. The next milestone should be a small, dependable, teachable game loop. Players currently cannot consistently trust shutdowns, advertised capacity, trace counterplay, persistence, or the accessibility controls. Those failures weaken every decision built on them.

The Factorio influence is visible in physical transport and bottlenecks. More depth should come from inspectable transport, reliable throughput, allocation and composed recipes. Factorio's own designers discuss finite belt throughput and blocked-item behavior; those are useful reference points, not a requirement to copy its implementation. [Factorio throughput](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-276), [Factorio transport behavior](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-176).

The Constructor ambition needs outside actors, contested opportunities and consequences. The current static city and contract board supply a starting point. If “RIM simulator” means RimWorld-like simulation, characters, needs and incidents would be a later layer. RimWorld's official description places characters, relationships and selectable storytellers at its center; RIM currently supplies none of that character simulation. A lightweight operator layer is a more manageable first experiment than a full colony system. [RimWorld official overview](https://rimworldgame.com/).

Accessibility has promising foundations: shape-coded resources, motion controls, contrast and an alternate palette. Implementation gaps currently stop those settings from delivering the advertised experience. The evaluation uses readable/scalable text, redundant visual cues, coherent input and controllable interaction as benchmarks consistent with the [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/). Headless layout and input probes are evidence of particular defects; they do not establish usability with low-vision, motor-impaired or screen-reader users.

| Area | Current strength | Main constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Core fantasy | A recognizable city-wide botnet and physical packet flows | Instructions and simulation rules disagree in several places |
| Automation | Sources, converters, upkeep, lossy links, fan-in and throttling | Throughput accounting is incomplete and nominal limits are unreliable |
| Construction | Road routing, footprints, coverage and district rules | Geometry, placement and visible targets sometimes disagree |
| Progression | Six resources, research, Cash target and contracts | Most advancement is optional; repeatable stock rewards can bypass the factory |
| Threat | Recoverable quarantines, sweeps, Trace and blackout | Counterplay does not reliably affect an active sweep; reload resets threats |
| UX | Sidebar, inspector, alerts, tooltips and graph | Hidden information, modal input leaks and 720p overflow |
| Accessibility | Several useful settings and resource shapes | 200% text fails for most UI, map palettes diverge, menu motion persists |
| Simulator depth | A functional network economy | No autonomous population, rival economy or character-story layer yet |
| Replay | Random city, visible seed and post-win continuation | One ruleset and one banked-Cash goal |

**Modes: what exists and what to build**

New Game and Continue are session actions. Pause is a timing state. Keep Playing continues the same simulation after victory. They do not currently provide distinct game modes. There is **one run ruleset**: a random 100×100 city, 80 starting Cycles, real-time simulation with pause, and a 1000-Cash goal.

| Mode | Status | Recommended purpose and rule changes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard operation | Current ruleset, after correctness fixes | Preserve scarce resources and recoverable ICE pressure; teach a reliable opening and make the result dependable |
| Guided operation | Proposed N01 | A curated city and short tasks that introduce production, delivery, selling, upkeep, overload and recovery |
| Sandbox | Proposed N02 | Optional threat, adjustable starting resources and unrestricted experimentation; label results separately |
| Scenario challenge | Proposed N03, later | Fixed seed/rules and a distinct objective such as a quiet extraction, limited Cash budget or Crypto quota |

Build Guided and Standard first. Add Sandbox once rules/configuration can be shared safely. Challenge content should follow stable saving and trustworthy metrics. A Spire heist (N04) can supply an eventual end objective without requiring a separate simulation engine.

**Evidence and limits**

**R** = reproduced with a headless engine probe or observed in its serialization output. These are targeted fixtures and UI/state tests, not a completed human playthrough. **C** = the finding is supported by directly inspected code; its ordinary-player manifestation still needs regression testing. **D** = a design or communicated-rule defect whose resolution depends on intended mechanics. B03's soft capacity, B08's district restriction and B25's repeatable rewards are explicitly marked this way; their behavior is observable, but the desired rule needs a design decision.

**P0** = can permanently block progress. **P1** = fix before the next public playtest because correctness, persistence, core interaction or readable access is affected. **P2** = meaningful consistency, diagnostics or polish issue. Counts: 1 P0, 37 P1, 12 P2; 30 runtime-backed and 20 source-backed findings, with three findings also requiring an explicit design-rule decision.

Missing features are kept in the additions list. Related symptoms are combined where they share a cause: ICE affordability and the unpaid refund are one finding; severing and rerouting a sweep are one finding; missing threat-save fields are one finding. Save omissions affecting packet value, paid boosts, contracts and cumulative results are separate because each loses a different player-facing operation.

Do not schedule all fifty tweaks before a playtest. The first useful slice is recovery + saving + readable/focused interaction + trustworthy network behavior, followed by a short Guided operation. Rival economies, multi-output routing and character simulation carry much larger scope and should follow evidence that the core loop is worth extending.

**Concrete acceptance checks**

- Round-trip a save containing fractional packet values, a paid boost, active sweep, quarantine/blackout, accepted and completed contracts, RNG state and run metrics. State and future behavior should resume consistently.
- Load an earlier save and then undo: no refund or state change may come from the discarded timeline. Reject malformed/version-incompatible saves without overwriting the running session.
- Execute an intentionally poor legal opening, including the three-Market/one-link case. Verify that the emergency recovery mechanism can restore a Cycles chain.
- Measure producer and bidirectional processor traffic against the chosen capacity model. Attribute loss to its real cause, and maintain resource accounting through conversion and rerouting.
- Disable a junction and a Market. Forwarding, processing, costs, trace and visual states must follow the documented control semantics.
- Launch a sweep, then sever, reroute, go quiet or use DDoS. Each response must have the exact effect stated to the player. Warning time should precede its stated damage.
- At 1280×720, 200% text and a full three-contract board, verify that every action is visible or scrollable. With Settings open, Escape dismisses the correct layer and Enter/E/Delete do not activate background gameplay.
